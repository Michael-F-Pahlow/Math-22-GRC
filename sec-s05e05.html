    Section 5.5 Episode 5: Square Roots

    Episode 5 Learning Goals.

    • Interpret square roots
      🔗
      🔗
    • Calculate very simple square roots by hand.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Calculate more complicated square roots on a calculator.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Episode 5 Key Terminology.

    • Square root
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Episode 5 Key Symbols.

    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{\quad} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \text{as in}\,\, \sqrt{16} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Remember that squaring a number means raising it to a power of 2.
    \begin{equation*} \text{square}\,\, 7 = 7^2 = 49 \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Definition 5.5.1.

    The square root is meant to undo or reverse the square.
    \begin{align*} \text{square}\,\, 6 \amp = 36 \\ \text{square root}\,\, 36 \amp =6 \end{align*}
    The symbol for the square root is
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{\quad} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    If we want to write something that means, “calculate the square root of 36,” we would write the 36 underneath the root symbol:
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{36} = 6 \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Example 5.5.2.

    Calculate the square root \(\sqrt{100}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution: Because \(10^2 = 100\text{,}\) we know:
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{100} = 10 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 5.5.3.

    Calculate the square root \(\sqrt{25}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution: Because \(5^2 = 25\text{,}\) we know:
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{25} = 5 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Not all square roots work out to be whole numbers and should be calculated on a calculator:
    🔗

    Example 5.5.4.

    Calculate the square root below. Round to 2 decimal places if necessary.
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt{7} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    There’s no nice whole number that becomes 7 after it’s squared. So, we calculate this square root on a calculator:
    \begin{align*} \sqrt{7} \amp = 2.64575131106…\\ \amp \approx 2.65 \,\,\text{after rounding} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Notice Something Neat!

    The number 7 is between 4 and 9. So \(\sqrt{7}\) is between \(\sqrt{4}=2\) and \(\sqrt{9}=3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    The geometric object called a square is a special kind of rectangle whose length and width are equal:
    🔗
    Use the variable \(s\) to represent the length and the width of the square.
    🔗
    Because of the rectangle area formula, the area of the square is the length, \(s\text{,}\) multiplied by the width, also \(s\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} A = s\times s = s^2 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    So, squaring the side length gives us the square’s area. That means taking the square root of the area gives us the square’s side length:
    \begin{equation*} s=\sqrt{A} \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Example 5.5.5.

    A square has an area of 10 square feet. Find the square’s side length. Round to 2 decimal places, if necessary.
    🔗
    Solution: We can find the side length by taking the square root of the area:
    \begin{align*} s \amp =\sqrt{10} \\ \amp = 3.162277660168… \\ \amp \approx 3.16 \,\,\text{feet} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
