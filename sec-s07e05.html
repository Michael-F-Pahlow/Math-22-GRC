    Section 7.5 Episode 5: Multiplying with Variables, Part 2

    Episode 5 Learning Goals.

    Use distribution to multiply a polynomial by a polynomial.
    🔗
    🔗
    How to Multiply a Polynomial by Another Polynomial:
    1. Distribute every term in the first polynomial to every term in the second polynomial.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Complete all the multiplication.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Combine like terms.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.5.1.

    Simplify:
    \begin{equation*} (x+3)(x+4) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution: We need to distribute every term in the first polynomial to every term in the second polynomial. To help organize this, we can make a small table.
    🔗
    We can put the terms of the first polynomial in the first column, the terms of the second polynomial in the first row. Then, we can fill up the rest of the table with the products:
    🔗
    \(x\) \(4\)
    \(x\) \(x\cdot x = x^2\) \(x\cdot 4 = 4x\)
    3 \(x\cdot 4 = 4x\) \(3\cdot 4 = 12\)
    Now, we add together all the terms in the table:
    \begin{equation*} x^2+4x+3x+12 \end{equation*}
    Finally, combine like terms:
    \begin{equation*} x^2+7x+12 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Makes Sense, Right?

    Imagine a rectangle with a width of \(x+3\) units and a length of \(x+4\) units.
    🔗
    On the one hand, the area of the rectangle is going to be its length times its width,
    \begin{equation*} (x+3)(x+4) \end{equation*}
    At the same time, the area of the rectangle is going to be the areas of all the little rectangles, added together:
    \begin{equation*} x^2+3x+4x+12 \end{equation*}
    This is the reason why distribution works.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.5.2.

    Simplify:
    \begin{equation*} (x^2+x-4)(x-3) \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution: We need to distribute every term in the first polynomial to every term in the second polynomial. We can organize this in a table, being careful to include the \(-\) signs.
    🔗
    \(x\) \(-3\)
    \(x^2\) \(x^2\cdot x = x^3\) \(x^2\cdot (-3) = -3x^2\)
    \(x\) \(x\cdot x = x^2\) \(x\cdot (-3) = -3x\)
    \(-4\) \((-4)\cdot x = -4x\) \((-4)\cdot (-3) = 12\)
    Now add together all the terms in the table:
    \begin{equation*} x^3-3x^2+x^2-3x-4x+12 \end{equation*}
    Finally, combine like terms:
    \begin{equation*} x^3-2x^2-7x+12 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 7.5.3.

    Simplify:
    \begin{equation*} (x+4)^2 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution: Remember that exponents mean repeated multiplication:
    \begin{equation*} (x+4)(x+4) \end{equation*}
    Now, we distribute:
    🔗
    \(x\) \(4\)
    \(x\) \(x\cdot x = x^2\) \(x\cdot 4 = 4x\)
    \(4\) \(4\cdot x = 4x\) \(4\cdot 4 = 16\)
    Now, we add together all the terms in the table:
    \begin{equation*} x^2+4x+4x+16 \end{equation*}
    Finally, combine like terms:
    \begin{equation*} x^2+8x+16 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
