      🔗

    Episode 6 Key Terminology.

    Slope-intercept form of a line
    🔗
    🔗
    An equation whose graph is a straight line can be written in the form:
    \begin{equation*} y = mx +b \end{equation*}
    The letters \(m\) and \(b\) stand for numbers that tell you the shape and position of the line:
    • The number \(m\) is the line’s slope
      🔗
      🔗
    • The number \(b\) comes from the line’s y-intercept \((0,b)\)
      🔗
      🔗
    This “template” for the equation of a line is called “slope-intercept” form.
    🔗

    Example 9.6.1.

    A line has the equation \(y=3x+2\text{.}\) Find the line’s slope and the line’s y-intercept. Then draw the line’s graph.
    🔗
    Solution: The slope “m” is the coefficient of \(x\) in the equation. Here, the coefficient of \(x\) is 3, so the slope is 3.
    🔗
    The “b,” related to the line’s y-intercept, is the number added in the back of the equation. Here, the number added in the back is 2. So, the y-intercept is the point (0,2).
    🔗
    Now let’s draw the line.
    🔗
    Then, use the fact that the slope is \(3=\frac{1}{3}\) = rise/run . From the point (0,2), go over 1 unit and then go up 3 units, and mark another point at this new location.
    🔗
    Now draw a straight line through the two points you marked:
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 9.6.2.

    A line has the equation \(2y+4x=6\text{.}\) Find the line’s slope and y-intercept.
    🔗
    Solution: The line is not currently written in slope-intercept form. It can be converted to slope-intercept form by solving for y (see Season 07, Episode 10: Rearranging Formulas).
    \begin{align*} 2y+4x\amp =6\\ 2y+4x-4x\amp =6-4x\\ 2y\amp =6-4x\\ \frac{2y}{2}\amp = \frac{6-4x}{2}\\ y\amp = \frac{6}{2}-\frac{4x}{2}\\ y\amp =3-2x\\ y\amp =-2x+3 \end{align*}
    Now we can see that the line’s slope is \(m=-2\) and its y-intercept is (0,3).
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 9.6.3.

    Find the equation of the line graphed below
    🔗
    Solution: Start by identifying the graph’s y-intercept. This is the point where the graph crosses the vertical axis.
    🔗
    The y-intercept is (0,1), which tells you the “b” in the slope-intercept template is \(b=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Now determine the slope of the line. Mark two easy-to-read points on the line and measure the rise and the run between them.
    🔗
    The run is 2 units and the rise is -1 units. Notice that the rise is negative because the graph goes down as we follow it from left to right. So the slope is
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{2}{-1} = -2 \end{equation*}
    Now we put our values of \(m\) and \(b\) into the slope-intercept form template:
    \begin{align*} y= \amp mx+b \\ y= \amp -2x+1 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
