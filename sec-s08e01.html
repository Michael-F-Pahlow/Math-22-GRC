    Section 8.1 Episode 1: Solving Inequalities, Part 1

    Episode 1 Learning Goals.

    • Interpret inequality notation.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Solve simple inequalities.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Episode 1 Terminology.

    • Inequality
      🔗
      🔗
    • Compound Inequality
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Episode 1 Key Symbols.

    \begin{equation*} \ge \quad\quad \text{as in}\,\, A\ge 2000 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \le \quad\quad \text{as in}\,\, T\le 32 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    The symbol \(\ge\) “greater than or equal to.” Think of it as a fusion of the \(\gt\) symbol and the \(=\) symbol.
    🔗
    For example, suppose the variable A represents our income, and we have $2000 worth of bills to pay. Then our income needs to be greater than or equal to $2000, enough money to pay our bills and hopefully a little more:
    \begin{equation*} A\ge 2000 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The symbol \(\le\) “greater than or equal to.” Think of it as a fusion of the \(\lt\) symbol and the \(=\) symbol.
    🔗
    For example, suppose the variable T represents the temperature of ice (in degrees Fahrenheit). As long as the temperature of the ice is 32 degrees or colder, the ice won’t melt:
    \begin{equation*} T\le 32 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    A line of math containing \(\gt\) ,\(\ge\text{,}\) \(\lt\) or \(\le\) is called an inequality.
    🔗

    Notice Something Neat!

    Any statement containing an inequality can be “reversed” as long as you make sure that the “mouth” of the symbol points toward the greater or warmer value.
    \begin{equation*} A \ge 2000 \,\,\text{is the same as}\,\, 2000 \le A \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} T \le 32 \,\,\text{is the same as}\,\, 32 \ge T \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    It’s also possible to have a line containing multiple inequality signs. These are called compound inequalities.
    🔗
    For example, if \(H\) represents the temperature in your house (in degrees Fahrenheit), a comfortable temperature might be between 68 and 74. Then we could write:
    \begin{equation*} 68 \le H \le 74 \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Be Careful!

    The symbol \(\lt\) means “less than”. When we write something like \(x\lt 5\) that means that \(x\) cannot be equal to 5. It has to be strictly less than 5.
    🔗
    By contrast, the symbol \(\le\) means “less than OR equal to”. When we write something like \(x \le 5\text{,}\) that means \(x\) could be less than 5 or it could also be equal to 5.
    🔗
    🔗
    Inequalities containing a variable \(x\) can be solved using almost exactly the way we solved equations.
    🔗
    How to Solve Inequalities
    1. If necessary, distribute.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Add or subtract terms to cancel out all the variables on one side of the equation.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Clean up the equation by combining like terms.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Add or subtract terms to cancel out all the numbers on the other side of the equation.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Clean up the equation by combining like terms.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. If necessary, finish isolating the variable using multiplication or division
      1. If you multiply or divide by a negative number, flip the inequality symbol
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Example 8.1.1.

    Solve the inequality
    \begin{equation*} 5x+12 \le 2x \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution 1: Subtract \(2x\) from both sides to move all the variables to the left:
    \begin{equation*} 5x+12 - 2x \le 2x -2x \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 3x+12 \le 0 \end{equation*}
    Subtract 12 from both sides to move all the numbers to the right:
    \begin{equation*} 3x+12-12\le 0 -12 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 3x \le -12 \end{equation*}
    Divide both sides by 3:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{3x}{3} \le \frac{-12}{3} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} x \le -4 \end{equation*}
    In other words, any x-value less than or equal to -4 will make the inequality true.
    🔗
    Solution 2: Subtract \(5x\) from both sides to move all the variables to the right:
    \begin{equation*} 5x+12 - 5x \le 2x-5x \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} 12 \le -3x \end{equation*}
    Divide both sides by -3. Since we’re dividing by a negative number, we need to flip the \(\le\) sign.
    \begin{equation*} \frac{12}{-3} \ge \frac{-3x}{-3} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} - 4 \ge x \end{equation*}
    Notice that we get exactly the same answer as we got before: Any x-value less than or equal to -4 will make the inequality true.
    🔗
    🔗
